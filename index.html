SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & METHODOLOGY

How It Works: The 7-Stage Physics Pipeline

From uncalibrated monocular camera feed to physics-informed gesture recognition. Click each pipeline stage to inspect the underlying biomechanics, algorithms, and formulas.

01

Video Ingestion & Landmark Extraction

MediaPipe Tasks Vision extracts 21 3D coordinates \(\mathbf{P}_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i)\) at 60 FPS.

Monocular RGB video frames are fed into a lightweight convolutional detector and regression network. Each hand is represented by 21 anatomical landmarks spanning the wrist, metacarpophalangeal (MCP), proximal interphalangeal (PIP), distal interphalangeal (DIP), and fingertip joints.

Output: Matrix \(\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{21 \times 3}\) normalized relative to wrist origin \(\mathbf{P}_0\).
02

Bone Vectors & Anatomical Joint Angles

Directional vectors between adjacent joints define 15 interior joint flexion/extension angles.

Bone segments are computed as vector differences \(\mathbf{b}_{ij} = \mathbf{P}_j - \mathbf{P}_i\). The interior joint angle \(\theta_j\) between parent bone \(\mathbf{b}_{ij}\) and child bone \(\mathbf{b}_{jk}\) is calculated using the dot product formula:

Formula: \(\theta_j = \arccos\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}_{ij} \cdot \mathbf{b}_{jk}}{\|\mathbf{b}_{ij}\| \|\mathbf{b}_{jk}\|}\right)\)
03

Trajectory Smoothing (Low-Pass Filter)

Suppression of high-frequency camera jitter via Butterworth / Exponential Smoothing.

Raw computer vision landmarks experience sensor noise and quantization error. Differentiating raw signals directly amplifies noise in acceleration and jerk. We apply a zero-phase low-pass digital filter:

Smoothing: \(\hat{\mathbf{P}}_i(t) = \alpha \mathbf{P}_i(t) + (1-\alpha) \hat{\mathbf{P}}_i(t-\Delta t)\) where \(\alpha \approx 0.65\).
04

Kinematic Higher-Order Differentiation

Numerical estimation of Velocity, Acceleration, and Jerk over timestamp intervals \(\Delta t\).

Using accurate hardware timestamps from `requestAnimationFrame`, we compute continuous kinematic derivatives for key anatomical markers (wrist, index tip, thumb tip):

Velocity: \(\mathbf{v}(t) = \frac{\Delta \mathbf{P}}{\Delta t}\) | Acceleration: \(\mathbf{a}(t) = \frac{\Delta \mathbf{v}}{\Delta t}\) | Jerk: \(\mathbf{j}(t) = \frac{\Delta \mathbf{a}}{\Delta t}\)
05

Rigid-Body Dynamics & Energy Formulation

Center-of-Mass (CoM) tracking and instantaneous kinetic energy \(KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2\).

Utilizing Dempster (1955) anthropometric body segment data, the human hand is modeled as a rigid body with mass \(m \approx 0.45\text{ kg}\) and moment of inertia tensor \(\mathbf{I}\). Center of mass \(\mathbf{P}_{cm}\) is computed across anatomical segment mass fractions:

Kinetic Energy: \(KE_{total} = \frac{1}{2} m \|\mathbf{v}_{cm}\|^2 + \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{\omega}^T \mathbf{I} \boldsymbol{\omega}\)
06

Physics-Informed Feature Vector Assembly

Concatenation of static geometric descriptors with dynamic thermodynamic features.

The system constructs an enriched feature vector \(\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{98}\) per time step:

\(\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{L}_{geom} (63), \boldsymbol{\theta}_{joint} (15), \mathbf{v}_{key} (9), \mathbf{a}_{key} (3), \mathbf{j}_{key} (3), KE (2), SPARC (1), \boldsymbol{\omega} (2)]\)
07

Gesture Disambiguation & Classification

Classifying sign categories using both structural shape and dynamic trajectory energy.

The feature vector is evaluated against calibrated decision boundaries. Dynamic twin pairs that share the exact same spatial skeleton (e.g. "Wait" vs "Halt!") are cleanly disambiguated because the abrupt gesture produces a distinct jerk spike and kinetic energy signature.

SANCHAR SAATHI & FIOL ACCESSIBLE RESEARCH CONTEXT

The Biomechanical Thesis

Standard sign recognition treats gestures as sequences of static poses. However, natural human sign languages (ASL, ISL, BSL) embed semantic meaning into movement dynamics: speed signifies urgency, deceleration indicates grammar boundaries, and abruptness changes verb mood. By incorporating physical quantities into machine learning, computers gain a physical understanding of human gesture dynamics.

Scientific Integrity & Limitations

Video-derived forces and torques (\(F \approx m \cdot a\)) are inertial approximations based on standard anthropometric mass tables (Dempster, 1955). They do not represent direct measurements of internal tendon strain or surface electromyography (EMG). Our prototype demonstrates that these kinematic approximations still provide sufficient discriminative power for gesture disambiguation.
